03 / SetupBefore you start

Five things that decide whether this program works, none of which are workouts. Tick them off as you go.

How every week is shaped

Six training days, Sunday always off (an optional 15-minute mobility session is listed, and it doesn't count). Two full-body barbell sessions every single week — Monday and Thursday, never the day before Saturday. Core work sits on both lift days, on Wednesday, and at the end of Friday's run. Around that: three swims, two or three runs, two bikes, and Saturday's anchor — the long ride plus a run straight off the bike.

Every fourth week is a recovery week — Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32 and 36 — where distances shrink by about a third, hard efforts go easy, and each lift drops a set but keeps its weight. Adaptation happens during the easy week, not the hard ones.

04 / LanguageHow to read it

Every workout is written as an effort level plus a set notation. Learn these once and the whole program becomes readable.

The effort scale

This program uses RPE — Rate of Perceived Exertion, a 1–10 self-rating of how hard something feels. No heart-rate monitor needed. The talk test is the check. In the gym, RPE 8 means "two more reps were possible".

RPE in the water

You can't do a talk test while swimming, so use your breathing. Easy / drills (2–3): breathing every third stroke feels comfortable the whole way. Steady (4–5): every third stroke still works, but you're glad when the wall arrives. Race pace (5–6): by the last quarter of the interval you want to switch to every second stroke. Hard (7): every second stroke from the start.

Drills are always 2–3. If a drill leaves you winded, you're swimming it, not drilling it.

Reading a workout line

4 × 50, 15s rest — swim a 50, rest 15 seconds, repeat until you've done four. That's one set; total distance 200.

6 × 50 as 25 drill / 25 swim — each 50 is split. First half is the drill, second half is normal freestyle.

4 × 6 min at RPE 6–7, 3 min easy — six minutes of work, three minutes spinning easy, four times through. The easy part isn't optional.

Back squat 3 × 5 — three working sets of five reps at the same weight, after warm-up sets. 2 × 8 per leg means both legs get eight in each set.

Every session has the same four parts: warm up (always easy) → drills or skill work → the main set → cool down. Short on time? Cut the main set, not the warm-up.

Yards vs. meters

Read this before your first pool session

Nearly every US pool is 25 yards long, and pool workouts here are written in yards. The race is in meters, which are about 9% longer:

400 m ≈ 450 yd ≈ 18 lengths  ·  800 m ≈ 875 yd ≈ 35 lengths  ·  1500 m ≈ 1,650 yd ≈ 66 lengths

These round up on purpose, so a distance you hit in the pool is one you can definitely cover in the lake. If your pool is 25 meters, swim the meter numbers as written.

05 / TestingYour paces

"Goal pace" appears throughout this program. It isn't a guess — it's a number you measure in Weeks 2, 11, 23, 31 and 38. Each test replaces a normal session; it never adds to one.

The three time trials

Swim — 400 yd for time. Warm up 10 min easy, then swim 16 lengths as steadily hard as you can hold. In Week 2 swim 200 yd instead if 400 is out of reach — that's information, not failure.

Bike — 20 min for distance. On the trainer: 10 min warm-up, then 20 min at the hardest effort you could hold for exactly that long. Weeks 11, 23 and 38.

Run — 5K for time. Flat route, 10 min easy warm-up, then 3.1 miles as hard as you can hold. In Week 2 run one mile instead.

Do the tests on different days. Log each one from its session card and it lands here.

Pace calculator

Filled in from your latest logged tests; type over them to experiment.

Swim race pace is your test pace plus 5–8 seconds per 100 — the race swim should feel easier than the test. Run race pace is your 5K pace plus 45–60 seconds per mile: about 30 seconds for the extra distance, about 20 for the fact that you just rode 25 miles. Bike race effort is RPE 4–5 — a pace you'd bet money you could hold for 90 minutes.

10 / ProofCheckpoints

Nine moments where you find out if it's working. Each asks only for something the preceding weeks trained you to do, and none land on a recovery week.

If you can't hit one, hold the phase an extra two or three weeks rather than pushing on. Losing three weeks costs you nothing; getting hurt costs you the race.

11 / RealityIf life happens

It will — work, travel, a cold. The program survives that if you cut in the right order.

Protect these three

The Saturday ride and brick, the long swim, and the Tuesday key run. These carry the race. Everything else is negotiable.

Cut in this order

1. Friday easy swim → 2. Monday easy swim → 3. Friday run → 4. Wednesday bike → 5. Thursday barbell → 6. Monday barbell. If you can only lift once, make it Monday.

Never double up

Don't make up a missed session by cramming two in later. A missed workout costs you nothing; two crammed together costs you a week.

Back from illness

Three days off sick? Return at about 60% for two or three sessions before resuming the plan. The weights come back faster than you fear.

Pain that changes how you move

Sharp joint pain means stop and get it looked at, not push through. Ten months is enough runway to lose two weeks and still be fine.

Two bad Saturdays running

Usually under-fuelling, under-sleeping, or a skipped recovery week. Check those three before assuming you're unfit.

Behind on the table?

Repeat the week you're on rather than skipping ahead. The numbers are a ladder, not a calendar. The one exception is the taper: whatever week you're on when July 3 arrives, switch to Week 42.
